MODEL SCOPE ↓
WHAT THIS MODEL COMPUTES+

The model integrates rigid-body angular motion and uses a local-patch phenomenological drag approximation. Its camera estimate is synthetic; the rendered arm is not a full constrained IK solution. Magnetic rotation is visual and does not drive the torque calculation.

Outputs are simulated, not measured. The display does not establish real stopping times, braking efficacy, tracking accuracy or capture safety. Spacecraft reaction, thermal effects and orbital dynamics are not modeled. Detumbling does not remove the target from orbit.

The sections below distinguish the proposed architecture from the current implementation.

THE ARCHITECTURE

THREE CONNECTED QUESTIONS.

01 / OBSERVATION

KNOW
THE MOTION.

Track a changing viewpoint. Estimate how the target turns. Carry uncertainty into every decision.

CAMERA & STATE ESTIMATION+

Intended architecture. Calibrated images and matched features can constrain relative pose. Metric position requires known geometry, scale or an independent depth measurement. Estimating spin also requires accounting for camera and servicer motion.

In this model. The camera measurement is synthesized from the simulated truth quaternion with added noise. It is not an image-processing or computer-vision pipeline, and its display does not establish tracking performance.

Occlusion, target symmetry, blur, lighting and stale measurements remain real design constraints.

02 / ROBOTIC POSITIONING

PLACE
THE SOURCE.

Bring the magnet to a useful pose. Geometry sets where the arm can reach; interaction physics sets what that pose can do.

ARM GEOMETRY & CONTROL+

Intended architecture. A controller requests a source position and orientation. Inverse kinematics would translate that pose into joint angles while respecting reach, joint limits, collision clearance and slew limits.

In this model. A two-link arm is rendered alongside Cartesian source motion. This is not a complete constrained inverse-kinematics solver or a validated robot trajectory.

The source’s actual pose and motion must feed any physical interaction model. Reaching a position alone does not establish that it will brake the target.

03 / MAGNETIC INTERACTION

CHANGE
THE SPIN.

A changing magnetic field can induce currents in a conducting target. The resulting torque depends on the interaction—not rotation alone.

TORQUE MODEL & LIMITS+

Intended architecture. The motor rotates a transverse magnetic dipole so its direction changes. Induced currents interact with the field; torque depends on geometry, material, frequency and relative motion. Arbitrary magnet rotation can add energy.

In this model. Angular motion is integrated numerically using rigid-body Euler equations and a quaternion update. Braking comes from a phenomenological local-patch drag model. The displayed magnetic phase does not enter the torque calculation, so the rotating field graphic is illustrative.

No measured efficacy or physical stopping time is established. Spacecraft reaction, thermal effects and orbital dynamics are not modeled. Reducing spin does not remove an object from orbit.
